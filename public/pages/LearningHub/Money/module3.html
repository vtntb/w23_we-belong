What’s a bank account?

A bank account is a financial account between a bank customer and a financial institution.

A bank account can be a deposit account, a credit card, or any other type of account offered by a financial institution.

Why do I need one?

  • Bank accounts are safe places to keep your money.
  • Your employer needs a bank account to pay your salary into.
  • Other payments, for example Centrelink benefits, also need to be paid into a bank account.
  • Money sitting in a bank account can earn interest.

Types of bank accounts

  • Savings Account
  • Cheque Account
  • Credit Account

How do I open a bank account?

You will need an application form from the bank.

You will need to complete a 100 point check for identification.

  • Birth certificate = 100 points
  • Student ID = 40 points
  • Medicare card = 25 points

How can I access my money?

  • Visit the bank
  • Automatic Transaction Machines (ATM)
  • Internet Banking
  • EFTPOS

Checking bank records

It is important to check your bank records and ensure they are correct as there may be errors including:

Incorrect deposits and withdrawals.

Unauthorised access on your account.

How to check a bank statement

Keep details of deposits and withdrawals.

Check the date, amount, interest, fees, deposits and withdrawals.

Keep records for taxation and Centrelink.

Keep all bank statements in a hardcopy file or save them online.

Example

  • Description = who paid you money or where funds were taken out of your bank account.
  • Withdrawals = is the money taken out of your account.
  • Deposits = this is the money placed into your account. Deposits are usually called Credits.
  • Balance = money left in your bank account.

Types of bank accounts

  • Add up all yearly expenses and divide the amount by the number of times you are paid each year.
  • Establish a budget and set up your pay so it automatically goes into separate accounts.
  • Keep a record of all spending in notebook, diary or spreadsheet.
