Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money you save to cover urgent or unexpected costs such as:

  • Car repairs
  • Unexpected travel
  • Urgent medical bills

An emergency fund can save you from using your credit card.

Set Up a Bank Account

Create a bank account without having a card linked to it. This will prevent you from accessing these emergency funds. All banks offer these accounts.

Set up automatic transfers that send money to your emergency bank account after you get paid.

Watch the CommBank video on how to transfer funds.

Beware of ATMs

Use only the ATMs of your bank or credit union.

While major banks have removed ATM fees from accounts that are not their own, there are some ATM machines that may still charge you a transaction fee.

To avoid being charged, make sure you visit your bank to take out the funds you need.

Create a Savings Plan

People with a savings plan are twice as likely to save successfully.

Start small with a short-term goal. Save small amounts first.

Once you reach your goal, you’ll have created a habit of saving you can be proud of. You’ll be able to keep going strong with a new goal.

Tax Refunds

Every year in July, we prepare tax returns to be sent to the Australian Taxation Office.

Refunds may be given after tax returns have been processed. If you receive a refund, put that amount into an emergency fund.

What are some other things that give you a big money break?

Save Loose Change

Save your loose change. Some banks will round up your items of food, payments or money they take from you, so why shouldn’t we do the same?

You can also use a piggy bank or empty tin of tomatoes if you like. Every cent counts!

The Envelope Method

Find an envelope and place 10% of your money in it that you use for everyday spending.

Everyday spending means a hot chocolate, petrol for the car, travel expenses and random purchases.

Example: If you get paid $300, put away $30, which is 10%, for everyday expenses.

Buy Generic Brands

Go generic. Ask your doctor or chemist if generic prescription drugs are a good option for you. Generic drugs can cost several hundred dollars less annually than brand-name drugs.

Example: Panadol and Nurofen can be bought as paracetamol and ibuprofen for $3–$4 cheaper.

Also look for generic brands while shopping at the supermarket.

Have a Meal Plan

Plan meals in advance and stick to a list while grocery shopping.

Choose meals that can be saved for leftovers.

Choose something you enjoy eating!

The 24 Hour Rule

The 24 hour rule helps avoid purchasing expensive or unnecessary items on impulse.

Think over each non-essential purchase for at least 24 hours.

If you don’t really need or want the item, don’t buy it!

Money as Work Time

Calculate purchases by hours worked instead of cost.

Take the amount of the item you’re considering purchasing and divide it by your hourly wage.

If it is a $50 pair of shoes and you make $10 an hour, ask yourself if those shoes are really worth five long hours of work.

Getting Out of Trouble

  • Freeze your credit. Call your bank and ask them to put a stop on your credit card until your finances are sorted.
  • Take one debt at a time. Find the debt with the highest interest rate and tackle that one first.
  • Speak to an expert such as a bank manager or financial planner to help you put a plan in place.
