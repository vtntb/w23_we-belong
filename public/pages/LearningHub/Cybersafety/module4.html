Learning Hub > Cybersafety > Module 4 – Cyberbullying: What to Do if It’s Happening to You?

Keep Calm

If you are having issues with your friends or someone else, whether it be at school or in other places, try not to react to them via email, text, or social networking sites. It’s easy to say something hurtful when you’re feeling angry or upset, but if it’s written down or posted online, it’s permanent. Nothing online is ever private, even after you delete it.

Talk to Someone

If someone writes something or posts a photo that upsets you, scares you, or makes you feel uncomfortable, talk to a friend, your parents, or another trusted adult. You don’t have to deal with hurt feelings or embarrassment on your own. The sooner you tell someone, the sooner something can be done about it and you can feel safer.

Record Evidence

Take screenshots or print off bullying messages or posts. Record the times and dates of any harassing phone calls.

Ask the Person to Delete It

If someone has posted something offensive about you, often the easiest thing to do is to ask them to delete it. You can tell them that their actions could be a crime.

Report It to the Website

You can report cyberbullying and have content removed if it violates the website’s terms of use, which is the agreement between a user and the owner of the website.

Report It to Your Phone Company

If you’re getting upsetting calls or texts, you can make a complaint to the phone company. The phone company may be able to trace the caller or sender and send them a warning letter. If the bad behaviour doesn’t stop after repeated warning letters, the offender’s phone number could be suspended or cancelled. For more information, contact your phone company, for example Telstra, Optus, or Vodafone.

Block the Bully

Most websites let you block problem users from contacting you. There are also apps available for some phones that let you block calls and texts from certain people.

Talk to Your Teacher, School Counsellor, or Principal

Most schools have anti-bullying guidelines that cover cyberbullying. If the person who is bullying you is a student at your school, then your school may be able to help you work things out.

Staying Out of Trouble

Think carefully before posting or sending. Remember that online actions have real-life consequences, and that anything you say can be saved and shared with others. If you have pretended to be someone else online, it is usually best to apologise, take down any offensive material as soon as possible, and talk to a trusted adult.

Reporting Bullying to Your School

If someone has been physically violent, threatened you, or sexually harassed you, you can report this to the police. If your bully is over 10 years old, they could be charged with criminal offences.

If you have been threatened or physically harmed, you can report it to the police.

If the bully has physically harmed you or made threats to physically harm you, they may be charged with assault.

If the bully has indecently assaulted you, you can also report it to the police.

If your things have been damaged or stolen, you can also report it to the police.

If the bully took away your things against your will or damaged your things, they can be charged with theft or damage to your property.

Generally, for people under 18, the police may give a warning or caution for bad behaviour. Imprisonment is only a last resort and is usually saved for cases where people repeatedly cause very serious harm, such as serious injury.

Report Bullying to Police

  1. Lodge a formal complaint with your school. The school has a legal duty to do something about bullying if it is happening at school. If telling someone is not enough to stop the bully’s behaviour, you can make a formal complaint to the school. Ask your parents or someone you trust to help make the complaint with you, especially if you are scared or worried about it.
  2. Make a complaint to the Department of Education. If you’ve complained to the school but they haven’t done anything, you can make a complaint to the Department of Education office. To make a complaint, you and your parents may need to fill in a form. In your complaint, you or your parents will have to provide detailed information about incidents and show why you think your school has failed to make the bullying stop.
