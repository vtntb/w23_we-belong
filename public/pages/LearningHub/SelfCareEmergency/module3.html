Signs That I Need Extra Help or Support

It can take some people longer than others to feel safe or back to their old self following a significant event.

There might be signs you need some extra support to help you feel better again.

They include:

  • Not wanting to go out and do things
  • Not wanting to be around other people
  • Being extra jumpy or sensitive
  • Having a short temper or getting frustrated easily
  • Seeing images of the emergency repeatedly in your mind
  • Trouble sleeping or having regular nightmares
  • Having no energy or being tired all the time

Mental Health Is on a Scale

  • Mental health exists on a scale.
  • Our mental health can change over time based on what we experience or are facing.
  • It’s important to pay attention to your mental wellbeing, observing how you feel and your level of stress.
  • Seeking help when we are on the lower end of the scale is important.
  • Self-care is important when on the higher end of the scale.

Where Can I Reach Out for Help?

  • Your GP or doctor
  • Mental health professionals such as psychologists, psychiatrists and counsellors
  • Support workers and Support Coordinators
  • Family or friends

For people who need support with depression, anxiety or suicidal thoughts:

Reaching out for help is a positive step. You do not need to wait until things feel too hard. Talking with someone early can help you feel supported, safe and understood.

If you are in immediate danger or need urgent help, call emergency services.

  • Lifeline provides 24-hour crisis counselling, support groups and suicide prevention services. Call 13 11 14, text 0477 13 11 14 or chat online.
  • Suicide Call Back Service provides 24/7 support if you or someone you know is feeling suicidal. Call 1300 659 467.
  • Beyond Blue aims to increase awareness of depression and anxiety and reduce stigma. If you or a loved one need help, you can call 1300 22 4636, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, or chat online.
  • MindSpot is a free telephone and online service for people with anxiety, stress, low mood or depression. It provides online assessment and treatment for anxiety and depression. MindSpot is not an emergency or instant response service. Call 1800 61 44 34.
  • Head to Health gives advice and will connect you to local mental health services. Call 1800 595 212.
  • If you’re feeling lonely, FriendLine supports anyone who’s feeling lonely, needs to reconnect or just wants a chat. You can call them 7 days a week on 1800 424 287, or chat online with one of their trained volunteers.
  • Kids Helpline is Australia’s only free 24/7 confidential and private counselling service specifically for children and young people aged 5 to 25. Call 1800 55 1800.
  • Headspace provides free online and telephone support and counselling to young people 12 to 25 and their families and friends. Call 1800 650 890, or chat online.
