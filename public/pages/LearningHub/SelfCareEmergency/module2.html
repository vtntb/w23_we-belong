  • Take deep breaths: Slow, deep breathing can help reduce stress and anxiety. Inhale slowly through your nose, hold for a few seconds, and then exhale slowly through your mouth.
  • Focus on what you can control: In an emergency, many things may be out of your control. Concentrate on actions you can take immediately to protect yourself and others.
  • Use positive self-talk: Remind yourself that you are capable of handling the situation. Phrases like “I can do this” or “I’m prepared” can help bolster your confidence.
  • Stay informed: Understanding the situation can reduce fear and anxiety. Use reliable sources to get accurate information about what is happening and what you should do.
  • Practice emergency plans: Regularly practicing what to do in an emergency can help you feel more prepared and less panicked if one occurs.
  • Lean on your support network: Sharing your feelings with others can help. In an emergency, try to stay with a group or maintain communication with loved ones, if possible.
  • Limit exposure to sensational news: Continuous exposure to alarming news can increase anxiety. Get the information you need, then take a break.
  • Visualise calmness: Imagine a place or experience that makes you feel peaceful and calm. This mental escape can reduce stress.
  • Acknowledge your feelings: It’s natural to feel scared or anxious. Recognising your emotions can help you manage them more effectively.
  • Seek professional help if needed: After the emergency, if you find it difficult to cope, talking to a mental health professional can be beneficial.

Distracting yourself by counting can help you not focus on things that are making you scared.

You can do this in different ways

  1. Count slowly from 1 to 10
  2. Count down from 100 to 0
  3. Count by 3’s (3, 6, 9, 12)

Holding things can also bring a sense of comfort.

Examples of things you could hold are:

  • Stress ball
  • Favourite blanket
  • Favourite toy
  • Fidget toys
  • Someone else’s hand
  • A pet

Talking to ourselves positively – changing the way we think will help us feel more confident.

Say phrases aloud or in your mind such as:

  • I can handle this
  • I have faced difficult situations before and handled them
  • I will be safe if I listen and follow directions
  • Just take one step at a time
  • I am not alone, and I can ask for help
  • Just breathe and keep going

Which Strategy Is Best For Me?

It is good to practice these strategies regularly so you can use them when they are needed.

Every person is different and will have their own preference as to which strategy works best for them.

Think about which one works best for you or you can reflect or talk to someone about what strategy you like to use.
