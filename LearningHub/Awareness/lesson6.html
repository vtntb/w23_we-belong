Learning Hub > Disability Awareness Training > Lesson 6 – Etiquette & Language
  • Promote the principle of “people first” eg: a person with a disability.
  • Use terms such as “person who uses a wheelchair”, do not use outdated terms such as handicapped, crippled, wheelchair bound.
  • Portray and treat people with a disability in the same manner as you would people without a disability.
  • Avoid stereotypical or stigmatizing depiction of people with a disability.
  • Avoid phrases and words that demean people with a disability.
  • Avoid concentrating on a person’s disability.
  • Refrain from using terms such as ‘the deaf’, ‘the blind’ or ‘the disabled’. These terms are inappropriate because they do not reflect the individuality, equality or dignity of the person with a disability.
  • Some people rely on their arms for balance, avoid touching them even if your intention is to assist.
  • Avoid touching wheelchairs, scooters or canes. People with a disability consider their equipment part of their personal space.
  • It is OK to say “It’s good to see you” or “see you later” to a person with visual impairment or is blind.
